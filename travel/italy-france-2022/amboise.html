Amboise & Chenonceau

Amboise anchored this stretch of the Loire, its royal château looming over the town from every angle — the Tour de l'Horloge marking the entrance to the pedestrian streets below, Anne of Brittany's ermine banners still flying from the rooftops, and a stone bridge carrying the road across the river itself. Day trips fanned out from there: Château Gaillard, just outside town, hides its orangery in the tuffeau hillside and its Jardins du Roy behind it, the site of France's very first Renaissance garden. Further along, the Château de Chenonceau spans the river in the postcard shot everyone comes for, its own gardens laid out in swirling parterres and quiet pergolas. Back in Amboise, the evenings ran to wine and beer — a troglodyte cellar's worth of old winemaking equipment at the Maison des Vins d'Amboise, and a flight of local ales at Art is an Ale to close out the day.
