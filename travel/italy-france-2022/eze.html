Eze & the Côte d'Azur

Eze rewards the climb: the Nietzsche Trail's steep stone steps — the same former mule path the philosopher climbed daily in 1883, reportedly finding inspiration for "Thus Spoke Zarathustra" along the way — lead up through the old town, jasmine and bougainvillea spilling over every wall, to a hilltop village that looks much as it must have centuries ago. At the top, the Jardin Exotique d'Èze fills the ruins of an old château with cacti and succulents, framing one of the best views on the whole coast — the sea spreading out far below, boats anchored along the rocky shoreline. Down at the water's edge, Anjuna keeps the day going late, a Balinese-style beach club built right into the cliffside. Day trips fan out from there: the lighthouse at the tip of Cap Ferrat, reached along a coastal path that slips past grand villas half-hidden in the pines, and Villefranche-sur-Mer's ochre-faced Église Saint-Michel just next door. Inland, Renoir's own bronze Venus Victrix still stands in the citrus grove at Les Collettes in Cagnes-sur-Mer, and Saint-Paul-de-Vence holds its own layered history — the house where the poet Jacques Prévert lived in the 1940s, the Collegiate Church's bell tower rising over the rooftops, and a worn stone stairway winding through the old town. Back in Eze, a bottle of Château La Coste rosé closes out the day, and the terrace goes quiet as evening settles over the water.
