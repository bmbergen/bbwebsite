Dublin

Dublin was the trip's opening chapter — a city where centuries of history sit comfortably alongside a lively, present-tense buzz. The Ha'penny Bridge arcs gracefully over the Liffey, one of the city's most photographed crossings and a natural starting point for a walk along the river. A few streets over, Temple Bar draws the crowds, its cobblestone lanes strung with lights and spilling over with flower baskets outside pubs that never seem to close. The Brazen Head, tucked away nearby, claims the title of Ireland's oldest pub — established in 1198 — and still pours pints beneath the same low ceilings that have hosted centuries of conversation. Across town, Kilmainham Gaol tells a very different story: a former prison whose grand, light-filled central hall belies the grim history within its cell blocks, each heavy iron door a reminder of the revolutionaries once held behind it. It's a city that moves easily between solemn and celebratory, sometimes within the same afternoon.
