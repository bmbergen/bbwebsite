Rome

Rome rewards wandering as much as any checklist — a night might start with cocktails at The Gin Corner and end with scooters parked beneath a bougainvillea-draped archway in Trastevere, its ivy-covered alleys busy with cafe tables long after dark. By day, the ancient city asserts itself everywhere: the Colosseum's ruined arena floor and weathered arches, the Arch of Titus with its carved menorah looted from Jerusalem, and the Arch of Septimius Severus rising over the Roman Forum's scattered columns. The Pantheon's columned portico still bears Agrippa's original inscription two thousand years on, while the Trevi Fountain draws crowds to its turquoise pool regardless of the hour. Up on the Pincio terrace, Rome shows its quieter side — a bust of Leonardo da Vinci watching over the gardens, a goose patrolling the pond by the Temple of Aesculapius in Villa Borghese, an old dog making his rounds against a skyline of domes, and, on one lucky afternoon, a bride crossing the terrace with St. Peter's floating in the haze behind her. Baroque Rome holds its own too: Bernini's Triton blows water skyward in Piazza Barberini, and the ceiling of Chiesa Nuova dissolves into painted clouds and saints. It's a city where a five- minute walk can move from a wedding dress to a two-thousand-year-old ruin without missing a beat.
