Cinque Terre

The Cinque Terre are five villages stitched together along a stretch of Ligurian coastline too steep for much else, each one stacked in pastel-colored buildings between the cliffs and the sea. Vernazza, the most photogenic of the five, anchors the visit — its tiny harbor glows gold at sunset, sailboats drift past, and the bell tower of Santa Margherita d'Antiochia rises just above the rooftops, visible from the castle terrace bar where a glass of wine comes with a view over the whole cove. A cliffside path connects Riomaggiore's overlooks to the rest of the villages, while Manarola's terraced vineyards produce Buranco, a local DOP wine, alongside a Ligurian dinner under the olive trees. Monterosso al Mare offers the trail's most conventional beach — striped umbrellas lined up beside a lone sea stack — and Corniglia, perched highest above the water, has the best sweeping views back down the coast toward Manarola. Every village shares the same rhythm: laundry lines and geraniums in terracotta pots, boats bobbing below cliffside piazzas, and, at the end of the day, the same golden light settling back over Vernazza's harbor.
