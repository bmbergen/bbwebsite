Dubrovnik

Dubrovnik is the trip's grand finale — a walled Renaissance city so complete and so dramatic that it's easy to see why it's Croatia's most famous stop. The city walls themselves are the main event: a mile-and-a- quarter loop atop centuries-old fortifications, with the terracotta rooftops of the old town spilling out on one side and the Adriatic crashing against Fort Lovrijenac on the other. Below, the old town is dense with landmarks — the Rector's Palace and Sponza Palace lit up at night, the bell tower keeping time over the main street, and the statue of playwright Marin Držić, his nose rubbed bright gold by generations of visitors for luck. A cable car climbs Mount Srđ for a sweeping view over the entire peninsula and the island of Lokrum offshore, while at street level, life moves at a warmer, slower pace — cats sunning on stone steps, a glass of Pošip on a patio overlooking the rooftops, and the old port glowing gold in the morning light. It's the kind of place that's genuinely worth the hype — polished, historic, and still capable of a quiet moment if you catch it at the right hour.
