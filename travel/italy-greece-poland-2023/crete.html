Crete

Heraklion's Koules Fortress still guards the harbor entrance, a Venetian holdover with the Lion of St. Mark carved into its walls and an old iron anchor resting nearby. A few blocks in, the Morosini Fountain's lions keep flowing past an ornate Moorish-arched facade. A short drive out of the city, a bust of Minos Kalokairinos — the man who first broke ground at Knossos — stands watch over the palace's reconstructed red columns and sprawling ruins. Chania, on the island's western coast, centers on its Venetian harbor: a trinket boat strung with shells bobs near the water, the Orthodox Cathedral catches the morning light, and the Monument of the Hand remembers the roughly 200 lives lost when the ferry Heraklion sank in 1966. The harbor changes character by the hour — pastel by day, gold at sunset, a lone fishing boat rigged with buoys at sunrise. A few streets inland, Chania's old town is its own reward: a blue-framed door, alleys canopied in bougainvillea, a cat keeping watch, morning glories climbing a wall, hand-painted café furniture, and geraniums beside a wine barrel. The trip closes out in the countryside, at the Olive Tree of Vouves, gnarled and still bearing fruit after an estimated 3,000 years, and a prickly pear cactus in bloom.
