Madrid

Madrid centers on the Royal Palace, Europe's largest by floor area, its endless galleries hung with tapestries and crowned by frescoed ceilings, gilded thrones, and a banquet hall still set for state dinners. Just outside, an equestrian Philip IV rears up in Plaza de Oriente, and a short walk west brings the ancient Temple of Debod into view — a 2,200-year-old Egyptian gift, reassembled stone by stone in a city park. Gran Vía's grand hotels look almost as monumental: the Riu Plaza España fills the old Edificio España, and its rooftop bar gives the best vantage in the city, a sangria and passionfruit margarita in hand while the Palace and Almudena Cathedral spread out below. Nearby, the winged bronze crowning the Metropolis Building keeps watch over the boulevard, not far from Cibeles, Madrid's own goddess on her lion-drawn chariot, her fountain and palace lit gold at dusk. Down in the historic center, the produce stalls of Mercado de San Miguel spill fruit into the aisles, Casa de la Panadería's frescoed facade anchors Plaza Mayor, and Chocolatería San Ginés keeps the churros coming late into the night — best paired with a round of gin tonics and a walk through Plaza de Santa Ana, still lively after dark. Retiro Park closes the day out: the Fallen Angel, reputedly the only public monument to Lucifer anywhere, and the glass-domed Palacio de Cristal mirrored in its own quiet pond.
